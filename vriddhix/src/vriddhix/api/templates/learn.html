How a chart becomes a number

Every score VriddhiX shows decomposes into named parts, and every part is arithmetic over past bars. This page explains that arithmetic — and, more usefully, the ways a platform like this can be wrong without anybody noticing.

Volatility contraction

A base forms when a stock that has already advanced stops advancing and starts oscillating. What makes it interesting is not the sideways move itself but the shape of it: each pullback shallower than the last, on quieter volume. That is supply being absorbed.

The engine scores six things and weights them: the quality of the prior trend (no base without one — a "base" after a downtrend is just a downtrend pausing), how many contractions formed, how cleanly they tightened, whether volume contracted with them, where price sits relative to the pivot, and the stock's relative strength.

The pivot is not a target. It is the highest point of the most recent contraction — the level at which the stock would be making a new local high. It marks where the question gets answered, not what the answer will be.

A base moves through states: FORMING → NEAR_PIVOT → BREAKOUT → CONFIRMED, or FAILED at any point. States are never skipped. That constraint sounds pedantic until you realise the breakout row is what all the research is built on — skipping it would mean a confirmed pattern with no record of the moment it broke out.

Market structure: BOS and CHoCH

Strip the vocabulary away and market structure is a sequence of swing highs and lows. An uptrend makes higher highs and higher lows. When price closes above the most recent swing high, the structure has broken.

The same break carries two different meanings depending on what came before it. If the structure was already bullish, a break upward is a BOS — a break of structure, continuation. If the structure was bearish, that same break is a CHoCH — a change of character, the first evidence the trend may be turning. VriddhiX stores both in one table, because they are the same event shape and differ only in context.

Confirmation lag is the thing most backtests get wrong. A swing high is only a swing high once enough bars have printed after it without exceeding it. If the engine needs three bars of confirmation, then on the day the swing occurred nobody knew it was one. VriddhiX stores the date a swing became knowable separately from the date it happened, and history is read by the former. Reading by the latter hands a backtest information from the future and produces results that cannot be repeated in life.

An order block is the last opposing candle before the move that broke structure — the bar where the other side gave up. It is treated as a zone, and marked mitigated the first time price trades back into it. Mitigation only moves forward: a block that was mitigated does not become unmitigated because a later scan looked at a shorter window.

Fair value gaps

Three consecutive candles. If the third candle's low sits entirely above the first candle's high, price moved so fast that a band of prices never traded — an imbalance. That band is a fair value gap.

Gaps are tracked forward. Each one carries a fill percentage that is a running maximum: a gap that filled 80% and then saw price retreat stays 80% filled, permanently. Storing the current overlap instead would let a zone quietly un-fill itself, and a zone that can un-fill is a zone you cannot reason about.

A gap is INVALIDATED rather than filled when price closes decisively through the far side. The imbalance is not merely worked off at that point; it is gone.

Market regime

Regime answers "what is the market doing now" from five measured components: trend of the benchmark, breadth (how much of the universe participates), momentum, participation across sectors, and volatility. Each contributes a weighted share of a 0–100 score, which classifies into STRONG_BULL through STRONG_BEAR.

Two refusals matter more than the score. First, below a coverage threshold the engine returns nothing rather than a confident number computed from a biased sample — a regime derived from 60% of the universe is a regime for that 60%. Second, regime changes require hysteresis: a threshold must be crossed by a margin and held for days. Without that the state flips on noise, and anything filtering on regime churns for reasons that have nothing to do with the market.

When a threshold has been crossed but hysteresis has not yet released the change, that pending state is stored too. Otherwise "stable" and "about to turn" look identical on a later read.

Relative strength

Relative strength here is a percentile rank across the universe, blended over 1-, 3-, 6- and 12-month returns. A score of 95 means the stock outperformed 95% of the ranked universe — nothing more.

Percentile rather than a z-score, deliberately: Indian equity returns have fat tails, and a z-score assumes a normal distribution it does not have. One stock up 300% would distort every other z-score in the universe; it cannot distort a rank.

Each score carries the horizons that actually had data. A stock listed two months ago can be ranked on its 1-month return alone, and the row says so — a rank from one horizon is a weaker claim than one from four, and a reader has to be able to tell them apart.

Sector rotation

Sectors are aggregated equal-weight, not capitalisation-weight. Cap weighting would make a handful of index heavyweights the sector, and the question being asked — is this group broadly strong? — would be answered by two stocks.

Each sector lands in a quadrant by strength and momentum: LEADING (strong, improving), WEAKENING (strong, fading), IMPROVING (weak, rising), LAGGING (weak, falling). A sector with too few constituents to rank meaningfully gets no quadrant at all, and its strength reads null — "unranked" is not the same statement as "ranked last".

Reading a score

A setup score is a weighted sum of named components, and every component is shown. When a stock scores 78, you can see that the prior trend contributed 14 points of a possible 15, contraction quality 12 of 20, and so on. A score you cannot decompose is a number you are being asked to take on faith.

Grades (A+ through C) are buckets over that score. They are research classifications of measured behaviour. A grade is not a recommendation, and a high grade in a hostile regime is a high-quality setup in a hostile regime — the context is shown next to it precisely because it changes what the number means.

Five quiet lies

These are the failure modes that do not announce themselves. Every one produces plausible output, which is what makes them dangerous.

1. Look-ahead

Using information the moment did not have — a swing not yet confirmed, a close not yet printed, a statistic computed over the whole series. It never crashes. It produces a beautiful equity curve that cannot be reproduced with real money. VriddhiX tests for it by truncating history and demanding identical output.

2. Survivorship

Backtesting today's index constituents over ten years measures the stocks that survived to be in the index today. The failures were removed from the sample by the same process that made them failures. When point-in-time membership is unavailable, VriddhiX marks the run CURRENT_UNIVERSE and carries that warning into the results — the bias travels with the numbers instead of living in documentation nobody reads.

3. Survivor statistics

A hit rate over only the setups that worked. It happens by accident: failures get closed, archived, filtered from the default view, and the remaining rows look like the whole population. VriddhiX keeps failed breakouts as first-class rows with the same shape as successes, and no view drops them by default.

4. Silent zeros

A measurement nobody computed, stored as 0. Zero percent of stocks above their 100-day average is a dramatic statement about the market; "we did not measure that" is not a statement about the market at all. Rendered on a chart they are the same line. Every unmeasured value here is null, all the way through to the JSON.

5. Silent repair

A platform that quietly "fixes" a suspicious price produces a series nobody can reconcile against the exchange. VriddhiX flags a suspected split as suspected and leaves the data alone. Zero-volume bars are kept too — an NSE circuit halt is a real event, and deleting those bars would fabricate a gap that never existed.

Glossary

base
A consolidation after an advance, during which pullbacks get shallower.
pivot
The high of the most recent contraction — where a breakout would be confirmed.
contraction
One pullback-and-recovery within a base, measured as a percentage depth.
BOS
Break of structure. A close beyond a swing, continuing the existing trend.
CHoCH
Change of character. The same break, but against the prior structure.
order block
The last opposing candle before the impulse that broke structure.
FVG
Fair value gap: a band of prices skipped by a three-candle move.
mitigation
How much of a zone price has traded back into. A running maximum.
breadth
How much of the universe participates — advancers, new highs, share above each average.
regime
A classification of current market conditions. A description, never a forecast.
hysteresis
Requiring a threshold to be crossed by a margin and held, so a state does not flicker.
relative strength
Percentile rank of blended returns against the ranked universe.
MFE / MAE
The best and worst excursion after entry — how far a trade actually travelled.
point-in-time
Resolving index membership as it was on a past date, not as it is today.
